My Journey to Happiness

By: Missy Nacario

September 9, 2026

  • 1.What is true happiness?
  • 2. Can we find happiness even during difficult times?
  • 3. How do our struggles and challenges shape our understanding of happiness?
  • 4. Do we need to achieve our dreams before we can truly be happy?
  • 5. What does my journey to happiness mean to me?


"I stopped searching for happiness and started creating it, one chapter at a time."


My story began long before I came to Canada. When I was eighteen, I left the Philippines and moved to Taiwan. I was young, and although I had dreams for my future, I did not completely understand how difficult growing up away from home would be. I had to learn how to live independently, adapt to a different culture, communicate in another language, work, study, and make decisions for myself. I had to become responsible for my own future at an age when many people were still living close to their families. Taiwan became more than just a place where I lived. It became a place where I grew up.

I spent years there. I studied, worked, made friends, experienced failures, celebrated small victories, and slowly became the person I am today. There were difficult days, but there were also beautiful ones. I built relationships that became important parts of my life. I created memories that I will carry with me wherever I go. I learned how to take care of myself and keep going even when things were not easy. Taiwan became one of the most important chapters of my life because it was there that I learned how to stand on my own.
Leaving Taiwan was not easy. Sometimes people think that moving to another country is simply about getting on a plane and starting somewhere new. They do not always see the emotional weight behind that decision. Leaving meant saying goodbye to familiar places, familiar routines, friends, and a life that I had spent years building. I knew I wanted something more for my future, but knowing that I wanted change did not make saying goodbye any easier. There were moments when I wondered if I was making a mistake. I questioned whether I should stay where life was already familiar or take the risk of starting again somewhere completely new. But deep inside, I knew that if I never tried, I might spend the rest of my life wondering what could have happened if I had been brave enough to take the chance. And so, I chose courage. I chose Canada.

And so, I chose courage. I chose Canada.

Coming to Canada was one of the biggest decisions of my life. I arrived with dreams, but I also arrived with fears. I knew that I would have to begin again. I would have to build a new life, continue my education, make new connections, learn new routines, and find my place in an environment that was unfamiliar to me. At first, everything felt exciting. I saw beautiful places, experienced new surroundings, and realized that I was finally living in a country I had once only imagined. I felt grateful for the opportunity. But beneath the excitement was another feeling—homesickness. I missed the people I had left behind. I missed the familiarity of Taiwan. I missed having people around me who already knew me, my personality, my habits, my stories, and the version of me that existed before Canada. I began to understand that starting a new life does not mean you stop missing your old one. There were nights when I questioned myself. Was Canada really the right decision? Was my old life better? Did I leave something behind that I should have kept? Sometimes, hearing other peoples opinions made those questions even louder. But other people do not carry the same dreams, fears, sacrifices, and experiences that I carry.
I had to remind myself that I did not come here because my life in Taiwan was meaningless. I came here because I wanted to see what else life could offer me. I can miss Taiwan and still choose Canada. I can love the people I left behind and still build new relationships. I can be scared and still move forward. I can have doubts without making my decision a mistake.
